\subsection*{Question 1: MDP formulation (3 points)}
Let $\mathcal G=\{0,\ldots,n_x-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,n_y-1\}
\times\{0,\ldots,n_z-1\}$, with obstacle cells $O\subset\mathcal G$.
Drone $i$ has a known goal $g_i$. A simulator state contains its cell $p_i$
and reached flag $r_i$; include elapsed time $t$ for the finite step limit:
\[
 s=\bigl((p_i,r_i)_{i=1}^{N},t\bigr),\qquad
 s_0=\bigl((p_i^0,\mathbf1\{p_i^0=g_i\})_{i=1}^{N},0\bigr).
\]
The positions and reached flags are observed. A reached drone remains at its
goal and continues to occupy that cell. Success, collision and the step limit
terminate the episode; the planner assigns zero continuation value after
termination.

For altitude-changing flight, each intended move is
\[
 u_i\in\{-1,0,1\}^3\setminus\{(0,0,0)\},\qquad
 \mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,26^N-1\}.
\]
The supplied encoding is little-endian:
$a=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i B^{i-1}$, where $B=26$, or $B=8$ for fixed altitude.
There is no selectable hover action. Reached drones ignore their commands.

The known transition model samples each drone's raw target independently,
conditional on the joint state and intended actions, then checks joint
collisions. Writing $q_i$ for the supplied per-drone target distribution and
$F$ for its target-to-state/termination mapping,
\[
 T(s,a,s')=\sum_{y:F(s,y)=s'}\prod_{i=1}^{N}q_i(y_i\mid p_i,r_i,a_i).
\]
For an unreached drone, the intended direction has weight
$d/(d+\alpha)$ and every other physical direction has weight
$(1-d/(d+\alpha))/(B-1)$, where $d$ is the supplied distance-field value.
Out-of-grid directions receive zero weight and the remaining weights are
renormalised. Obstacle targets are not removed: hitting one ends the mission.
The code retains that drone's previous cell in the returned terminal state.
Two drones in the same returned cell also cause termination. Cell swaps and
diagonal segment crossings are allowed by the stated cell-occupancy model.

Use the provided transition reward, rather than an additional reward shaping
term. For step cost $c$, collision penalty $C$ and goal reward $G$,
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sample}}=Nc+C(C_{\mathrm{obs}}+C_{\mathrm{veh}})
       +G\sum_i\mathbf1\{r_i=0,\ r_i'=1\}.
\]
Here $C_{\mathrm{obs}}$ counts obstacle hits, and $C_{\mathrm{veh}}$ counts
all drones in shared-cell groups. The objective is
\[
 \max_\pi\ \mathbb E_\pi\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\gamma^tR_t\right],
 \qquad \tau=\min\{T_{\mathrm{success}},T_{\mathrm{collision}},H\}.
\]
Negative step costs favour fast completion; collision penalties discourage
failure. This expected-return objective does not guarantee zero collision
probability under stochastic motion.

\subsection*{Question 2: Approximate online solver (4 points)}
I use the lectures' generative-model UCT/MCTS, with guided action widening
for the exponentially large joint action set~\cite{mdp-lectures}.
Unlike A1's RRT-Connect, it evaluates stochastic outcomes and replans after
every observed transition. Full value/policy iteration enumerates too many
joint states; exact RTDP backups also require action and successor sums.
MCTS directly uses the supplied generative interface. Belief-space solvers
are unnecessary for this fully observed problem, while model-free RL would
require a separate training and generalisation study~\cite{tutorials,rl-lectures}.

The four components are specified as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Selection.} Choose an unvisited admitted action first; otherwise
      maximise
      \[
       Q(s,a)+0.35\sqrt{\frac{\log(N(s)+1)}{N(s,a)}}.
      \]
      Returns and heuristic values are divided by
      $\kappa=\max\{1,N(|G|+2|C|)\}$ so the exploration constant has a
      comparable scale across drone counts.
\item \textbf{Expansion.} Admit at most
      $\min\{|\mathcal A_{\mathrm{eff}}(s)|,
      \lceil1.5\sqrt{N(s)+1}\rceil\}$ actions. The first proposal is guided;
      later proposals use a uniform joint command with probability $0.15$,
      otherwise a randomised guided command. Duplicate proposals are retried
      at most 12 times. Commands of already reached drones are canonically
      encoded as zero. Each distinct sampled successor has its own child;
      stochastic outcomes are never replaced by the most likely outcome.
\item \textbf{Rollout.} At the first new successor, simulate up to eight
      steps: a uniform joint command with probability $0.10$, otherwise the
      deterministic guided command. The tree depth is capped at eight.
      Terminal continuation is zero; other cutoffs use the heuristic below.
\item \textbf{Backup.} With $z=R/\kappa+\gamma v$, update the sampled
      state--action edge by the incremental mean~\cite{tutorials}:
      \[
       N(s,a)\gets N(s,a)+1,\qquad
       Q(s,a)\gets Q(s,a)+\frac{z-Q(s,a)}{N(s,a)}.
      \]
      Increment $N(s)$ and repeat. Return the root action with most visits,
      breaking ties by $Q$; use the guided fallback if no simulation finishes.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Guidance and leaf estimate.}
For each goal, breadth-first search computes static free-cell distances
$d_i(p)$ using the physical eight/26 neighbours. This deterministic
relaxation ignores other drones and slips. Intended-action scores are
\[
 d_i(p_i+u_i)+0.025\|p_i+u_i-g_i\|_1
      +\frac{0.3}{\max\{1,d_{\mathrm{clear}}(p_i+u_i)\}}.
\]
Out-of-grid/obstacle targets receive cost $10^6$; unreachable targets use
distance cost $10^4$. Goal-occupied and already reserved target cells receive
an additional $10^5$. Process active drones in index order for the fallback
and in random order for stochastic proposals. The randomised proposal
samples among the best four commands within $1.05$ of the minimum, with
weights $\exp[-3(\mathrm{score}-\mathrm{score}_{\min})]$.
These preferences guide search; the simulator still determines all actual
collisions and rewards.

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt}
With $h$ steps remaining, let $D=\min\{h,\max_{i:r_i=0}d_i(p_i)\}$,
using $D=h$ if an active goal is statically unreachable. The leaf estimate is
\[
 \widehat V(s,h)=Nc\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}\gamma^j
       +G\!\sum_{\substack{i:r_i=0\\1\le d_i(p_i)\le h}}
                     \gamma^{d_i(p_i)-1}.
\]
It is zero after success or when $h=0$. This estimate approximates team
completion and one-time goal rewards; it omits future collision risk and
is not an exact value function. Maps are built once and their setup time is
reported separately. Above 150,000 grid cells the implementation uses a
Chebyshev-distance fallback and omits the clearance term to bound
preprocessing memory.
\end{minipage}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=pseudocode,caption={Budgeted UCT with sampled outcomes}]
PROCEDURE PLAN(s, budget)
    deadline <- NOW + max(0, 0.97 * min(budget, 10) - 0.002)
    fallback <- GUIDED(s); root <- empty node
    h <- H - number of previously executed decisions
    WHILE before deadline AND trials < 2000 AND h > 0:
        IF all drones reached: BREAK
        SEARCH(root, s, h, 0)
    RETURN most-visited root action (tie: greatest Q), or fallback

PROCEDURE SEARCH(node, s, h, depth)
    IF h = 0 OR all drones reached: RETURN 0
    IF depth >= 8: RETURN ROLLOUT(s, h)
    admit a new proposed action if widening permits
    a <- unvisited action, otherwise UCB-maximising action
    (s_next, r, done) <- SIMULATE(s, a) // check deadline first
    IF done: v <- 0
    ELSE IF this outcome child is new:
        create child; v <- ROLLOUT(s_next, h - 1)
    ELSE:
        v <- SEARCH(child, s_next, h - 1, depth + 1)
    z <- r / kappa + gamma * v
    increment edge visits; update Q by incremental mean
    increment node visits; RETURN z

PROCEDURE ROLLOUT(s, h)
    simulate up to min(8, h) epsilon-greedy guided steps
    check deadline before each simulator call
    stop immediately on success or collision
    tail <- 0 if terminal, otherwise Vhat(s, remaining) / kappa
    RETURN discounted sampled rewards plus discounted tail
\end{lstlisting}
Interrupted incomplete tree trials are discarded. The remaining horizon is
decremented in both tree search and rollout because the supplied
\texttt{simulate} does not enforce the execution step limit.

\subsection*{Question 3: Implementation and demonstration (8 points)}
The submission entry point is \texttt{run\_u8076655.py};
\texttt{planner.py} implements B2. The supplied simulator is vendored at
revision \texttt{ea3cbfe}, recorded with hashes in \texttt{UPSTREAM.json}.
Only Vedo imports are deferred to visualisation methods; a subclass disables
the plotting hooks for headless runs. AST comparison verifies that the
constructor, reset, action encoding, dynamics, rewards and execution methods
are unchanged. The original checkout is preserved.

The planner accesses only \texttt{get\_config()}, \texttt{simulate()}, and
the action-count interface, using its actual property form and also accepting
a callable form~\cite{provided-code}. Static heuristic maps are derived from
the public configuration. It never inspects a future random outcome or
changes an execution state during planning. Independent simulator instances
use seeds $s$ for execution and $1{,}000{,}003+s$ for planning; the proposal
RNG uses $2{,}000{,}003+s$. Tutorial Week~8's sampling-quality concern is
addressed with NumPy generators, while Week~9's incremental averaging is used
in the backups~\cite{tutorials}.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=terminal]

NumPy, SciPy and PyYAML are sufficient for headless execution; Matplotlib
generates figures. Vedo/VTK is optional for the viewer. There is no training
checkpoint or external data dependency. The default per-decision allowance
is 9.5 seconds, with at most 2,000 trials. Python search is serial and the
runner sets numerical-library thread limits to one.

The demo cases cover one fixed-altitude drone ($8$ actions) through four
altitude-changing drones and five obstacle cells ($26^4=456{,}976$ joint
actions). The latter is a stress case beyond the stated $<50$-action expected
test. Widening avoids constructing the full joint action list.

\subsection*{Question 4: Experimental investigation (20 points)}

\subsubsection*{Design and controls}
The main study is a balanced $3\times3\times3$ factorial design with two
drones. Starts $(0,1,1),(0,3,1)$, goals $(11,1,1),(11,3,1)$, $\alpha=0.25$,
$c=-1$, $C=-100$, $G=100$, $\gamma=0.98$, and $H=60$ are fixed.
Each of the 27 conditions uses seeds 0--19 and a 0.04-second planning
allowance per decision: 540 episodes.

For $F=36n_y-|O|$ free cells, $F^2$ is a positional state-count upper
bound, rather than an enumerated reachable-state count. The clock multiplier
is constant across conditions. Action masks restrict intended commands only:
each retained command calls the same original 26-direction transition
model, including its slip outcomes. This preserves retained transition
kernels; changing the simulator's altitude flag would not.

The state comparison tests whether global state count affects a focused
online search or mainly its map setup. Action widening is expected to limit
the cost of many joint commands, though added commands may improve recovery.
Clutter can increase detours and collisions; because the supplied noise
depends on obstacle distance, geometry and motion uncertainty change
together. Expanding the grid also changes distant boundaries, and action
masks change control capability. Thus these experiments estimate effects over
the specified problem families, not isolated universal scaling laws.

Four representative tasks additionally test the full solver, uniform
proposals/rollouts, zero leaf values, removal of widening, and guided control without
search (20 seeds per cell; 400 episodes). A budget study uses 0.02, 0.1 and
0.5 seconds on three tasks (10 seeds; 90 episodes). Eighteen regression
scenarios (20 seeds) and sixteen generated random layouts (10 seeds) cover
1--4 drones, 2D/3D movement, goals already occupied, narrow passages,
boundaries, strong wind, infeasibility and timeouts. Together the recorded
studies contain 1,550 executed episodes; they are not an exhaustive set of
all possible environments.

\subsubsection*{Metrics and confidence intervals}
Success requires all goals reached without collision within the step limit.
To prevent early collision from appearing fast, define penalised completion
length
\[
 L=\begin{cases}\tau,&\text{success},\\H,&\text{failure}.\end{cases}
\]
Report $L$, discounted return, success counts, map/setup time, planning time,
simulation calls and admitted root actions. Failures remain in the means.
For episode-level continuous metrics, use the two-sided Student-$t$ interval
\[
 \bar x\ \pm\ t_{0.975,n-1}\frac{s_x}{\sqrt n}.
\]
These mean intervals are approximate for mixed success/failure distributions.
Success proportions have Wilson 95\% intervals in \texttt{summary.csv}.
For factorial marginal means, first average the nine conditions within each
seed block, then form a $t$ interval across 20 blocks. High-minus-low factor
effects and ablation effects use paired seed-block differences. This retains
the dependence introduced by reusing seeds across conditions.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility and limits}
All scenarios, generators, raw CSVs, per-suite metadata, seed-0 trajectories,
summary CSV/JSON files and figure scripts are included in the source archive.
\texttt{course\_inventory.json} records the local course-material inventory;
\texttt{model\_verification.json} records the upstream-core comparison.
Wall-clock stopping means a seed alone need not reproduce an identical
search tree on a different run or machine. A fixed trial cap under a generous
time limit is used for the deterministic reproducibility test.

The tested noise range and finite scenario set do not establish performance
on every unseen layout. Sparse stochastic outcome trees, heuristic distance
errors, occupied goals and narrow passages remain important limitations.
Nonzero slip probability can make collision risk unavoidable; the supplied
finite penalty is not a hard safety constraint. More computation may reduce
estimation error, but does not guarantee a better individual outcome or an
optimal policy. Reported factor rankings apply only to the tested levels and
fixed reward/budget settings.
Conditions ran serially in fixed order on one machine; timing comparisons
also remain subject to machine load and thermal drift.
