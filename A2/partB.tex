\subsection*{Question 1: MDP formulation (3 points)}
Let $\mathcal G=\{0,\ldots,n_x-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,n_y-1\}
\times\{0,\ldots,n_z-1\}$, with obstacles $O$ and known drone goals $g_i$.
The finite-horizon MDP is $(\mathcal S,\mathcal A,T,R,\gamma)$.

\textbf{States and actions.} A live state is
\[
 s=\bigl((p_i,r_i)_{i=1}^{N},t\bigr),\qquad 0\le t<H,
\]
where $p_i$ is drone $i$'s cell and $r_i$ indicates that it has reached its
goal. Reached drones remain at their goals and occupy those cells.
The state space also contains an absorbing state $\bot$, entered after a
collision, all goals being reached, or the step limit $H$.
Each active drone commands one of $B=26$ neighbour offsets (or $B=8$ with
fixed altitude), giving $\mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,B-1\}^{N}$.
There is no hover action; reached drones ignore their commands.

\textbf{Transitions.} Let $q_i(y_i\mid p_i,r_i,a_i)$ be the supplied,
normalised single-drone target law (Appendix~\ref{app:guidance}).
For a reached drone, $q_i(y_i\mid p_i,1,a_i)=\mathbf1\{y_i=p_i\}$.
Raw targets $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_N)$ are conditionally independent.
Let $F(s,y)$ update positions and flags, increment $t$, and return $\bot$
on termination. Then
\[
 T(s,a,s')=\sum_{y:F(s,y)=s'}\prod_{i=1}^{N}
 q_i(y_i\mid p_i,r_i,a_i),\qquad T(\bot,a,\bot)=1.
\]
An obstacle hit or shared returned cell terminates the mission; cell swaps
and diagonal crossings are permitted by the supplied collision rule.

\textbf{Reward and objective.} For a sampled outcome $y$, let $K_O$ count
obstacle hits, $K_V$ count drones in shared-cell groups, and $\Delta r_i$
indicate a newly reached goal. The simulator returns
\[
 \widetilde r(s,a,y)=Nc_{\mathrm{step}}+C(K_O+K_V)+G\sum_i\Delta r_i,
 \qquad c_{\mathrm{step}}<0,\ C<0,\ G>0.
\]
Thus $R(s,a)=\mathbb E_y[\widetilde r(s,a,y)\mid s,a]$ and $R(\bot,a)=0$.
The terminal transition receives its reward before absorption. The objective is
\[
 \max_\mu\ \mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
 \sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\gamma^t\widetilde r_t\right],
 \qquad \tau=\min\{T_{\mathrm{success}},T_{\mathrm{collision}},H\}.
\]
This encourages fast completion and penalises collisions.

\subsection*{Question 2: Approximate online solver (4 points)}
I use UCT/MCTS with progressive widening,
re-planned after every executed step. The supplied generative model samples
outcomes without enumerating the state space or all $26^4=456{,}976$ joint
actions.

\textbf{Guidance: single-drone value iteration.} Before planning, each drone's
MDP is solved alone, ignoring the other drones, by finite-horizon value
iteration on the supplied slip law (Week 5 MDP-solving lecture, slide 9).
With per-drone reward
$r_i=c_{\mathrm{step}}+C\,\mathbf1\{\text{crash}\}+G\,\mathbf1\{\text{arrival}\}$,
\[
 Q_{i,h}(p,a)=\mathbb E\bigl[r_i+\gamma V_{i,h-1}(p')\mid p,a\bigr],\qquad
 V_{i,h}(p)=\max_a Q_{i,h}(p,a),\qquad V_{i,0}=0,
\]
for $h=1,\ldots,H$, where crash and arrival end that drone's problem.
$Q_{i,h}$ ranks commands and $V_{i,h}$ evaluates rollout cutoffs, both at
the number $h$ of steps actually left. Solving each drone alone is our
approximation for the joint MCTS, not a solution of the joint problem.
For a command from $p_i$ towards $q$ (action $a_q$) with $h$ steps left, the
score, in step-cost units and lower being better, is
\[
 -\frac{Q_{i,h}(p_i,a_q)}{\eta}+0.025\|q-g_i\|_1
 +\lambda\ell(p_i)\sum_{\substack{k\ne i\\r_k=0}}
 \sigma(p_k)\mathbf1\{q\in\mathcal N(p_k)\}.
\]
Here $\eta=\max\{|c_{\mathrm{step}}|,10^{-3}(G+|C|),10^{-9}\}$ is the
bounded step-cost unit; $\ell$ and $\sigma$ are the normalised intended and per-neighbour slip
probabilities, and $\lambda$ converts collision loss into step-cost units
(Appendix~\ref{app:guidance}). The last term is an inter-drone risk surrogate.
$\mathcal N$ contains physical neighbours at the allowed altitude.
Intended-target filtering removes commands whose intended cell is outside
the grid or an obstacle (the simulator would accept them), and frozen or
already reserved targets incur an additional $10^9$.
\textsc{Greedy} processes drones in index order and minimises this score;
\textsc{Sample} is a truncated softmax proposal: random drone order,
sampling among the four cheapest commands within $1.05$ of the minimum
with weights $e^{-3(\mathrm{score}-\min)}$.
\textsc{Explore} samples uniformly from free, in-grid, unreserved targets
when available. An earlier guidance (A) replaced $-Q_{i,h}/\eta$
by risk-weighted Dijkstra costs; development round~3 adopted value
guidance, and Appendix~\ref{app:guidance-compare} compares both on
S1, S2, S4, S5 and the additional validation suites.

Figure~\ref{fig:risk-maps} separates distance, slip risk and value. In the
cluttered map, the value-guided route leaves the high-risk block that the
step distance would cut through. Behind the wall, every cell carries the
unavoidable cost of crossing the one gap, and the saved run heads for the
gap. These examples explain the guidance; they do not establish a
performance improvement.

\subsubsection*{The four MCTS components}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Selection.} Try unvisited admitted actions first; otherwise maximise
\[
 Q(s,a)+c_{\mathrm{UCT}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(N(s)+1)}{N(s,a)}},
 \qquad c_{\mathrm{UCT}}=0.35.
\]
Returns are normalised by $\kappa=\max\{1,N(G+2|C|)\}$.
\item \textbf{Expansion.} The action limit is
$\min\{\lceil0.5\sqrt{N(s)+1}\rceil,\prod_{i:r_i=0}|U_i^{\mathrm{adm}}|\}$.
Here $U_i^{\mathrm{adm}}$ contains commands towards free, in-grid neighbours.
Admit \textsc{Greedy} first, then \textsc{Explore} with probability $0.15$
and the truncated softmax \textsc{Sample} otherwise (at most 12 attempts for
a new action).
Each distinct sampled successor has its own child.
\begin{samepage}
\item \textbf{Simulation.} A greedy rollout with $\varepsilon$ exploration
runs for at most eight steps, choosing \textsc{Explore} with probability
$0.1$, otherwise \textsc{Greedy}. Terminal outcomes have zero continuation
value; other cutoffs with $h$ steps left add $\widehat V(s,h)/\kappa$, where
the unnormalised heuristic leaf value is
\[
 \widehat V(s,h)=\sum_{i:r_i=0}V_{i,h}(p_i)
 +c_{\mathrm{step}}\,|\{i:r_i=1\}|\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}\gamma^j,
 \qquad D=\min\{h,\max_{i:r_i=0}\delta_i\},
\]
where the second term charges drones already parked at their goals the
step cost while others fly ($\delta_i$: breadth-first distance). A drone
that frozen goal occupants disconnect from its goal contributes
$c_{\mathrm{step}}\sum_{j<h}\gamma^j$ instead (checked on maps of at most
20,000 cells). Tree depth is capped at eight.
\par
\end{samepage}
\item \textbf{Backpropagation.} A sample-mean return backup with heuristic
leaf evaluation: back up $v=R_{\mathrm{sample}}/\kappa+\gamma v'$;
increment edge and node visits and update the edge mean and sample variance
using Welford's recurrence.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Root decision.} Let $a_g$ be the greedy action and $\hat\sigma^2$
the pooled within-action variance. Among visited actions, define
\[
 m(a)=Q(a)-Q(a_g)-z_{\mathrm{sw}}\hat\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac1{N(a)}+\frac1{N(a_g)}},\qquad z_{\mathrm{sw}}=1.
\]
Execute the action with largest positive $m(a)$; otherwise keep $a_g$.
With no completed trial, use $a_g$ directly.
This is heuristic switching based on an estimated standard-error margin;
it has no formal policy-improvement guarantee. Parameters, the guidance and
this root rule were selected on 16 separate development layouts;
executing the largest-mean-$Q$ action instead raised $L$ there
(Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}).

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=pseudocode,basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,caption={Online UCT; rollout and admission rules are defined above.}]
PROCEDURE PLAN(s, budget, K = 2000)
    deadline <- NOW + max(0, 0.97 * min(budget, 10) - 0.002)
    h <- H - t; a_g <- GREEDY(s, h); root <- empty node; sims <- 0
    WHILE NOW < deadline AND sims < K AND h > 0 AND not all reached:
        TRY: SEARCH(root, s, h, 0); sims += 1
        ON Deadline: BREAK
    RETURN root decision above, or a_g if no trial completed

PROCEDURE SEARCH(node, s, h, depth)
    IF h = 0 OR all reached: RETURN 0
    IF depth >= 8: RETURN ROLLOUT(s, h)
    admit an action under the expansion rule, if permitted
    a <- unvisited action first, otherwise argmax UCB
    (s', r, done) <- SIMULATE(s, a)  // check deadline before call
    IF done: v <- 0
    ELSE IF child(node, a, s') is new:
        create child; v <- ROLLOUT(s', h - 1)
    ELSE: v <- SEARCH(child(node, a, s'), s', h - 1, depth + 1)
    v <- r / kappa + gamma * v
    update visits, mean and variance; RETURN v
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}
\textsc{Rollout} discounts sampled rewards and the terminal/cutoff value
defined above. It may stop at the deadline and return a leaf estimate;
a deadline exception before a simulator call aborts that trial.
The planner tracks the remaining horizon because \texttt{simulate} does
not enforce the execution step limit.

\subsection*{Question 4: Experimental investigation (20 points)}
\subsubsection*{Experimental design}
The main evaluation uses 2,000 simulations per decision; S3 includes
ablations and a zero-search policy, and S4 varies the simulation cap.
Experimental runs use a relaxed wall-clock cap to isolate search effort;
the demo stops at the first of its time and trial limits. In the four
local single-process B3 runs the
2,000-trial cap ended $\val{DemoCapped}$ of $\val{DemoDecisions}$ decisions,
and the one clock-bound decision still completed $\val{DemoMinSims}$
simulations (largest decision $\val{DemoMaxPlan}$\,s). Slower hardware could
end decisions on the clock with fewer simulations; S4 finds no consistent
completion improvement from 16 to 2,000 simulations on four tasks.
Its intervals do not establish
equivalence or robustness to a binding deadline on other hardware.
Wall times logged during the parallel experiments reflect CPU sharing between
worker processes, not demo decision times. Conditions share execution seeds 0--19
unless noted, with comparisons paired within each scenario.
Development layouts were excluded from evaluation. Only independent
experimental episodes ran in parallel; planning within each episode was serial.

\textbf{Metrics.} With $F$ free cells, there are up to $F^N$ positional
configurations and $B^N$ joint commands.
Success means all goals reached within $H$ without collision.
Penalised completion length is $L=\text{steps}$ on success and $L=H$ on
failure. Success proportions have Wilson 95\% intervals; continuous means
have Student-$t$ intervals $\bar x\pm t_{0.975,n-1}s/\sqrt n$.
High-minus-low factor effects average the other factors within each seed;
component effects pair each ablation with the full solver before averaging
corners. Their 95\% intervals use seed blocks. Cross-suite guidance and
baseline differences instead use percentile bootstrap intervals from
20,000 resamples of whole execution-seed clusters, preserving dependence
across conditions; repeated conditions are counted once in the guidance
aggregate. Supporting means and
intervals are in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.

\textbf{Clutter causes the largest degradation within these tested levels.}
Its success effect is $\val{EffCP}$\,pp $\val{EffCPci}$; no factor's success
interval excludes zero, so the ranking rests on $L$ (clutter minus state
$\val{PairCS}$ $\val{PairCSci}$, clutter minus action $\val{PairCA}$

The solver succeeds in $\val{FactSuccess}\%$ of S1 episodes; all

\textbf{State-space padding has little observed effect.}
For $n_y=10$ versus 20, $\val{SameSTen}$ of
$\val{SameSTotal}$ pairs match in steps, return and outcome.
This tests off-route padding, not longer routes or additional drones.

\textbf{Richer commands shorten routes.}
Changing 6 to 26 directions reduces $L$; the success difference is unresolved.
The action--clutter interaction is $\val{IntAC}$ $\val{IntACci}$ steps,
consistent with diagonal/vertical moves easing obstacle
avoidance. The state--action interaction is also resolved
($\val{IntSA}$ $\val{IntSAci}$): richer commands help more in the wide
$n_y=20$ grid, plausibly because the boundary of the narrow grid truncates
lateral slips that diagonal commands would otherwise correct. The
state--clutter interaction is not resolved ($\val{IntSCci}$).

\subsubsection*{S2: geometry, noise and action branching}
At $n_y=10$, the clutter effect averaged over the two command sets is
$\val{ClutterNoisy}$ steps $\val{ClutterNoisyci}$ at $\alpha=0.25$,
versus $\val{ClutterQuiet}$ $\val{ClutterQuietci}$ at $\alpha=0.01$,
where one of 120 episodes fails. Thus geometry still adds detour cost, while
noise substantially increases the observed clutter penalty.
The supplied transition law raises slip probability near obstacles.

Duplicating command labels up to $|A|=10{,}816$ leaves success at 19/20 and
mean $L$ within 0.3 steps. The paired interval $\val{Branchci}$ includes
zero; failures occur on different seeds and contribute to its width. The admitted root-action
count stays near 23. This separates pure label branching from the extra control
authority in S1. Detailed controls, including the visit share of the
\emph{executed} action, appear in Appendix~\ref{app:controls}.
