The factorial covers two drones at $\alpha=0.25$, and S5 has only one layout
per $(N,\alpha)$ pair. State padding changes available space, and richer
commands change control authority; the ranking is specific to these levels.
The $t$ intervals for bimodal $L$ are approximate; all intervals are
pointwise, without correction for multiple comparisons.
Single-drone values ignore the other drones: the leaf checks disconnection
by frozen drones but not detours around them, and inter-drone risk enters only
through the proposal surrogate. Successful wall-gap runs average
$\val{SuppGapSteps}$ steps for an $\val{SuppGapRoute}$-step static route, against
$\val{SuppGapStepsA}$ with the risk-free cutoff estimate of guidance A. This is
consistent with value cutoffs removing hesitation before an unavoidable risky
passage (Figure~\ref{fig:risk-maps}, bottom right), although these
experiments do not isolate the cause.
Finite-horizon value iteration on the supplied model gives maximum success
probabilities of
$\val{CeilGap}\%$ for the wall gap and $\val{CeilCorridor}\%$ for the corridor
(Appendix~\ref{app:supplement}). Both lie inside the Wilson intervals of the
solver's $\val{SuppSuppSingleWallgap2D}$ and $\val{SuppSuppYieldCorridor}$
successes. This demonstrates that some slip risk is unavoidable,
but does not establish optimality or attribute every observed failure to it.
Outcome progressive widening or an open-loop tree are possible follow-up
designs for improving successor reuse; neither was evaluated here.
