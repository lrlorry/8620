\section{Algorithm details and model conventions}
\label{app:guidance}
For an in-grid cell $q$, let $\mathcal N(q)$ be its physical neighbours
and $V(q)=|\mathcal N(q)|$. For a command towards a free in-grid neighbour,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \pi(q)&=\frac{d(q)}{d(q)+\alpha},&
 M(q)&=\pi(q)+(V(q)-1)\frac{1-\pi(q)}{B-1},\\
 \ell(q)&=\frac{\pi(q)}{M(q)},&
 \sigma(q)&=\frac{1-\pi(q)}{(B-1)M(q)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The obstacle-slip surrogate is
$\rho(q)=|\mathcal N(q)\cap O|\sigma(q)$.
Dijkstra costs use $1+\lambda\rho(q)$ for entry into a non-goal cell and
1 for entry into the goal, where
\[
 \lambda=\frac{w(G+|C|)}
 {\max\{|c_{\mathrm{step}}|,\ 10^{-3}(G+|C|),\ 10^{-9}\}},\qquad w=1.
\]
With $w=0$, costs reduce to physical breadth-first distances.
At most 150,000 grid cells are mapped; larger grids use a Chebyshev-distance
fallback without risk maps. Frozen-goal connectivity checks are cached
by drone and reached subset on maps of at most 20,000 cells; larger maps
retain the static-distance estimate.

Grid boundaries are not obstacles. With no obstacles, the supplied
SciPy distance transform measures distance to a virtual point beyond a
grid corner, rather than returning infinity, so slips remain possible.
On an obstacle hit, the simulator retains the drone's old cell but returns
a terminal flag. The model in B1 maps that outcome to $\bot$; its sampled
reward is computed before this mapping. Joint commands are encoded
little-endian, $a=\sum_i a_iB^{i-1}$.

At a rollout cutoff after $L$ sampled steps, the normalised return is
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\gamma^j\frac{r_j}{\kappa}
 +\gamma^L\frac{\widehat V(s_L,h-L)}{\kappa},
\]
with zero continuation on terminal outcomes. For a backed-up sample $v$,
Welford's edge update is
\[
 n\gets n+1,\quad \Delta\gets v-Q,\quad
 Q\gets Q+\Delta/n,\quad S\gets S+\Delta(v-Q).
\]
The pooled root variance is
$\hat\sigma^2=\sum_a S_a/\sum_a(n_a-1)$ over visited actions, or zero
if the denominator is zero. This is a sample-based heuristic variance
estimate, not a confidence guarantee.
