Let
\[
x_t=(c_t,v_t,p_t^1,\ldots,p_t^n)
\]
denote the car's position and speed, together with the
positions of the $n$ pedestrians. The car chooses a
driving action $a_t$ (e.g., accelerate, maintain speed,
or brake), aiming to reach destination $G$ while
avoiding collisions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(a) Scenario suitable for an MDP.}

Suppose pedestrians follow known stochastic movement
rules that depend only on the current observable
configuration. For example, each pedestrian crosses
or continues walking according to probabilities
determined by their current position, without any
persistent hidden intention affecting future motion.

Since the car observes all state variables required
to predict the next state, the Markov property holds:
\[
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_{0:t},a_{0:t})
=
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t,a_t).
\]

Thus, an MDP with state $s_t=x_t$ and transition
function
\[
T(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})
=
P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)
\]
captures the stochastic pedestrian motion.
The state is fully observable, so belief-state
estimation is unnecessary. An MDP is therefore suitable.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(b) Scenario suitable for a POMDP.}

Now suppose each pedestrian has a persistent but
unobservable destination $g_i$, such as crossing
to the opposite side or continuing along the sidewalk.
Although the car observes pedestrian positions
accurately, their destinations are unknown.

Let $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$. The complete state is
\[
s_t=(x_t,g),
\]
but the car observes only $o_t=x_t$.

Different destinations can induce different
transition probabilities even at the same observed
configuration:
\[
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t,a_t,g_1)
\neq
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t,a_t,g_2),
\]
where $g_1$ and $g_2$ denote two possible joint

Consequently, the observed configuration alone
need not be Markov. The car must maintain a belief

\[
b_t(g)=P(g\mid x_{0:t},a_{0:t-1}).
\]

\[
P(x_{t+1}\mid b_t,x_t,a_t)
=
\sum_g
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t,a_t,g)b_t(g).
\]

As new pedestrian movements are observed, the belief
can be updated and used to select safe driving actions.
A POMDP is therefore more suitable because it explicitly
accounts for the hidden destinations and their
uncertainty during planning.
